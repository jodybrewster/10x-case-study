Future-state target architecture: single customer identity

A proposed identity layer across commerce, CRM, the member portal and the EHR, designed to resolve people carefully while keeping clinical data in the right systems.

Executive view

Create an identity registry that assigns an opaque, stable person_id and maintains governed links to records in e-commerce, CRM, the member portal and the EHR.

The registry is the authority for cross-system identity links and their evidence, not a replacement for each system's business records or source-specific contact data.

Keep buyer, recipient, registrant, patient and authorized representative as distinct roles because one transaction can involve several people.

Accept registrations even while identity is being resolved, but do not attach clinical results or disclose identity-dependent information until the patient link is confirmed.

Treat identity, contact details, kit registration and result-linkage data as sensitive and apply the stricter health-data controls wherever a record could reveal a person's relationship to testing or care.

Scope and evidence boundary

The case describes e-commerce, CRM, member portal and EHR systems, with a lab partner in the sample and results journey.

The local code is a Python CSV replay with an in-memory CRM stand-in, not a production REST service or a complete implementation of these system connections.

The e-commerce data is Shopify-like and the CRM IDs are Salesforce-style, but both vendor identifications are inferences and remain unconfirmed.

The proposed interfaces and controls below are target-state recommendations, not claims about current production capabilities.

See the current-system diagnosis for the evidence, observed data shapes and open production questions.

Target architecture

The integration layer should use the production-approved transport available for each system, such as APIs, webhooks, managed file exchange or change-data feeds.

The case does not establish which transport is available, so transport selection, authentication, delivery guarantees and freshness targets require discovery with the system owners.

Regardless of transport, normalize each incoming change to an idempotent event with a source, source record ID, event time, processing time and schema version.

Use an outbox, durable queue or equivalent retryable handoff where supported so a temporary downstream failure does not lose an accepted order or registration.

Use source-specific IDs as crosswalk keys, not as interchangeable person IDs.

Examples from the supplied files include e-commerce customer 7000001, order #100001, CRM account 001A000001, portal registration R000001, EHR chart CH050001 and lab result LR000001.

The IDs identify different entity types and have different owners; an order or kit ID identifies a transaction or specimen path, not necessarily the person who will register or receive a result.

Do not make email, phone, name plus ZIP, date of birth or a household address a global primary key.

Store contact attributes with source and time provenance rather than overwriting a person's one canonical email with whichever system sent the latest event.

An order should retain separate buyer, recipient and ship-to references where known.

A kit should retain its order relationship and a separate registrant relationship.

A portal registration should record the registrant and its kit; an EHR chart should be linked to the patient only after an authorized identity decision.

A patient can have multiple charts or results until enterprise chart reconciliation is explicitly validated.

Record matching and uncertainty

Evidence strategy

Resolve known source crosswalks first, such as an already-confirmed mapping from an e-commerce customer ID to a person ID.

Use exact, unique and appropriately verified identifiers as strong evidence, but check for collisions and source quality before treating them as conclusive.

Use name, date of birth, email, phone and address as supporting evidence with source, recency and verification status attached.

Treat shared household address, order email, ship-to name and a kit barcode as transaction evidence, not proof that buyer, recipient, registrant and patient are the same person.

Treat missing values as no evidence and placeholders such as 1920-01-01 as missing or invalid according to a documented source rule.

Never award a match because both records have the same blank email or phone.

Do not use a lab result, diagnosis or other clinical content as a general-purpose matching feature.

Decision policy

The match service should produce a ranked candidate list with a human-readable explanation, evidence provenance, confidence band and any hard conflicts.

Automatically confirm a link only when there is one sufficiently strong candidate, independent evidence supports it, no disqualifying conflict exists and the rule has been validated against labeled examples.

Route ambiguous, conflicting or below-threshold candidates to a restricted review queue.

Leave records unmatched when there is not enough evidence; an explicit unmatched state is safer than forcing a false link.

Do not set a universal numeric threshold until the business owners agree on the acceptable false-link risk and the threshold is measured on representative labeled cases.

The unused matcher_v1.py in the supplied code is not a safe production policy as written: it treats two missing values as a match and has no recorded evidence or review decision.

Identity-resolution state machine

Model the status of each identity link as a state machine, not as a single status for a person or customer account.

Every transition should preserve the previous decision and append the actor, time, reason and evidence used.

Corrections should supersede a link or record an unmerge rather than erase history.

Keep this identity-link state machine separate from order fulfillment, kit registration, lab processing, portal access and communication consent states.

Registration and result safety

Accept a valid kit registration and assign a registration ID even when its person link is unresolved.

Move the identity link to Review or Unmatched and allow operations to resolve it asynchronously without asking the customer to repeat a successful registration.

Keep the kit and registration connected while review is pending, but do not publish a result under a patient identity that is not confirmed.

Once the patient-to-chart link is confirmed through an authorized process, associate the result to the EHR chart and make it available through the portal's normal authorization controls.

If the EHR returns conflicting chart information, stop the release path and create a high-priority review case rather than selecting the first chart.

The registry should publish identity-link changes to subscribers or expose a controlled lookup interface, depending on the transport supported by each system.

Downstream systems should receive only the reference and status they need, not the full match evidence or clinical context.

Do not let an order email silently update the CRM's preferred email; reconcile contact points with source, verification and consent semantics.

Protected health information and privacy controls

Design conservatively as if identity mappings, kit registration and result-linkage metadata are protected health information because they can reveal that a person is testing or receiving care.

Keep clinical results, diagnoses and detailed health attributes in the EHR and approved clinical workflow.

Keep the registry's minimum necessary data to opaque identifiers, source references, decision status and evidence needed to support a safe link.

Where matching needs direct identifiers, process them in a tightly restricted matching boundary and store protected values separately from general operational logs.

Use keyed tokens for exact-match indexes where appropriate, with keys held in a managed secrets service and access separated from application operators.

For fuzzy matching, decrypt only the fields needed inside the restricted service, avoid writing raw values to logs, traces or review notifications and retain them only as long as the approved workflow requires.

Encrypt data in transit and at rest, apply least-privilege roles, require strong operator authentication and audit every search, view, decision, export and correction.

Limit the review queue to trained identity-resolution staff and show only the fields needed to decide the case.

Do not put patient identifiers or health-related match details in analytics, generic support tooling, email notifications or AI prompts unless a separately approved control set permits it.

Keep consent separate from identity confidence and from purchase or registration status.

Represent consent by channel and purpose with its source, timestamp and scope; do not infer opt-in from a purchase, kit registration or account merge.

When records are linked or merged, preserve all opt-outs and apply the most restrictive applicable communication state until conflicting consent evidence is reviewed.

Before production, confirm the organization's legal role for each data flow, the applicable privacy requirements, vendor contracts and any business associate agreement needs with counsel and privacy leadership.

Define retention, correction, deletion, breach response and data-subject request handling for both the source records and the identity crosswalk.

Operations and governance

Provide an operations view for pending reviews, stale cases, conflicting links, source delivery failures and result-release holds.

Require a reason code and evidence reference for every manual approval, rejection, merge or unmerge.

Monitor false-link rate, review queue age, registration completion, time from registration to confirmed patient link, result-release holds, duplicate-link rate and consent preservation.

Set service-level targets only after observing source delivery cadence and review capacity.

Establish named owners for identity policy, EHR patient-link approval, CRM data stewardship, consent policy, security operations and source-system contracts.

Phased introduction

  1. Discover: validate production systems, interfaces, identifiers, data ownership, consent meanings, patient-link authority and operational reviewers.
  2. Shadow: build crosswalk suggestions from representative data without changing source records or exposing results differently.
  3. Measure and tune: review labeled samples, quantify false links and unresolved cases, and set thresholds with clinical, operations and privacy owners.
  4. Pilot: enable confirmed identity links for a narrow workflow while registrations continue independently and result release stays gated on confirmed patient linkage.
  5. Expand: add system consumers gradually, with reconciliation, rollback and audit checks at each step.

Decisions to validate against the brief and production owners

  • Which system is authoritative for person creation, contact fields, patient-chart linkage and communication consent?
  • What production transport exists between each pair of systems, and what are its ordering, retry, latency and idempotency guarantees?
  • Which identifiers are verified, unique and stable in each source, and can any be reused or shared?
  • Who is authorized to confirm or correct an EHR patient link?
  • Can a portal registration be accepted before identity resolution, and what existing product behavior must be preserved?
  • Which result-ready signal, if any, may be exposed to a non-clinical marketing tool, and under what consent and privacy review?
  • What retention, correction, deletion and incident procedures apply to the central crosswalk and decision history?
