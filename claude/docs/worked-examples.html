Worked examples

Ten people, followed through the current system and the design

Each example is a real case in the supplied data. Together they cover the six reported symptoms and the matching problems the design has to solve.

Every name and record is fictional case data. Identifiers are shown so each example can be looked up in data/, identities.csv and review_queue.csv.

Overview

For each person the table gives four things: their records, what the current sync did, what the working prototype does today and what the target design would do. The current-system column comes from replaying legacy-sync in timestamp order, or from the CSV files where it says so. The prototype column is read from its output.

The prototype resolves identity only. Roles per kit, consent, human review and the event log exist as design, so the target-design column describes intended behaviour that is not built. The prototype's match scores are not calibrated probabilities and its accuracy has not been measured.

1. Tyler Williams

One person, three email addresses.

RecordsE-commerce 7000326, 7000327, 7000328. CRM 001A000213, 001A000497. Portal R000285, R000286. EHR CH050205.
Current systemEach new email made a new e-commerce customer. In the time-ordered replay his order under a new address matched his CRM account on surname and zip, then overwrote its email. Three days later he registered a kit with the old address, was not found and was rejected as a possible duplicate of himself. Symptoms 2 and 3.
Working prototypeAll eight records resolve to one person. The registrations and the chart join on the kit barcode at 1.0. The three e-commerce customers score only 0.858 against each other, below the threshold. They are joined through records that carry his birthdate. The weakest link is 0.943.
Target designOne person key. Every email is kept as an identifier with its source and date, with one marked preferred. A new email adds an identifier and never a new person.

2. Elizabeth and Thomas Hall

Two relatives at one address.

RecordsElizabeth: CRM 001A000417, e-commerce 7000500, two registrations, chart CH050306. Thomas: CRM 001A000652, e-commerce 7000416, registration R000356, chart CH050261.
Current systemThomas ordered with an email the CRM had not seen. The sync matched surname and zip, filed his purchase on Elizabeth's account and replaced her email with his. Two days later Elizabeth's registration was rejected as a duplicate. Her result arrived while her account still read as bought. Symptoms 1, 2, 3 and 5 in five days.
Working prototypeTwo persons, each with four or five records across all four systems. Different first names and different birthdates keep them apart. No review item is raised.
Target designTwo persons joined by a household relationship. A shared surname, zip or phone is recorded as evidence that they live together, which the matcher treats as a reason not to merge.

3. Kevin Adams and Kevin Adams

Same name, same address, different birthdates.

RecordsBorn 1970: CRM 001A000551, e-commerce 7000108, registration R000092, chart CH050061. Born 1998: CRM 001A000686, e-commerce 7000527, registration R000448, chart CH050321.
Current systemThe younger Kevin's account was matched for the older Kevin's order and took his email. When the older Kevin registered, the email lookup found the younger one's account, the birthdates differed and the registration was rejected as an identity conflict.
Working prototypeTwo persons. The birthdate veto separates every record that has a birthdate. The e-commerce records have none, so one review item at 0.858 asks whether the two are the same, backed by seven record pairs.
Target designThe veto holds with no exception for a likely typo. The reviewer answers once, a keep-apart rule is stored and the question is not asked again unless new evidence arrives.

4. Lisa and Joseph Harris

A gift that could not be handed over.

RecordsLisa, the buyer: CRM 001A000349, e-commerce 7000094. Joseph, the recipient: CRM 001A000350, registration R000459, chart CH050327.
Current systemLisa bought a kit as a gift. When Joseph registered it, the sync tried to move the purchase to his account. His account belongs to an inactive sales rep, the CRM refused the move and the whole registration failed. Symptom 4.
Working prototypeTwo persons, four records each. The slice resolves identity only and does not model the gift.
Target designThe kit carries three roles: Lisa as buyer, Joseph as registrant and patient. Nothing is moved between accounts and account ownership is never checked, so there is nothing to fail.

5. Sofia Thompson

A shared email and two charts.

RecordsCRM 001A000379, e-commerce 7000324, registrations R000282 and R000470, charts CH050203 and CH050332. Her relative Kevin registered R000468 with her email address.
Current systemKevin registered a gift kit using Sofia's email. The lookup found Sofia's account, the birthdates differed and he was rejected. Separately the EHR holds two charts for Sofia with the same name and birthdate.
Working prototypeKevin is a separate person even though his registration carries Sofia's email. Sofia's records form two groups, one per chart, with a review item at 1.0 because two charts are never merged by code.
Target designA shared email is household evidence. The two-chart question goes to clinical operations, who merge charts in the EHR. The identity service links both charts to one person only after that decision.

6. Anthony Turner, known as Tony

A nickname.

RecordsCRM 001A000482 under Tony. E-commerce 7000301, registration R000260 and chart CH050188 under Anthony.
Current systemNothing went wrong for him. He is here because a plain comparison of first names counts him as a mismatch. The first pass of this analysis read 23 such cases as misfiled purchases. About 9 were nicknames like this one.
Working prototypeOne person with four records. The nickname table maps Tony to Anthony before names are compared.
Target designThe nickname table is reference data with an owner. A nickname missing from it costs a review and never a false merge.

7. Matthew Anderson

Three CRM accounts and one opt-out.

RecordsCRM 001A000126 (opted out of texts and calls), 001A000487 under Matt, 001A000560 (not opted out). E-commerce 7000132.
Current systemThe merge utility copies a fixed list of fields that leaves out both consent flags. Merging into the account that is not opted out would delete his opt-out and he would be texted again. Symptom 6. This is read from the code, since the replay never merges.
Working prototypeOne person with four records. The slice does not process consent.
Target designConsent is a record per channel address. On a merge, opted out beats unknown and unknown beats opted in, so his opt-out survives whichever account is kept.

8. Jennifer Martin

The same mailbox written two ways.

RecordsE-commerce 7000316, 7000317. CRM 001A000163, 001A000491, 001A000618. Registrations R000273 to R000275. Chart CH050198.
Current systemHer address appears as jennifermartin83@ and j.ennifermartin83@, which made two e-commerce customers. In the replay her CRM email flipped between the two forms three times as she ordered.
Working prototypeOne person with nine records, the largest in the output. The dotted variant adds a small weight, with name, phone and birthdate doing the work.
Target designBoth forms are kept as identifiers. Variant rules are measured before they count towards an automatic link. A rule that has not been measured sends the pair to review.

9. Mary and Kevin Rodriguez

Purchases swapped between relatives.

RecordsMary: CRM 001A000354, e-commerce 7000011. Kevin: CRM 001A000355, e-commerce 7000034, registration R000461, chart CH050328.
Current systemIn the CRM file Mary's order #100021 sits on Kevin's account and Kevin's order #100050 sits on Mary's. Symptom 1, visible in the data without running anything.
Working prototypeTwo persons. Mary has no birthdate anywhere and no clinical records, so she is two records. The slice ignores which account an order was filed on unless the full name agrees.
Target designEach purchase belongs to its buyer. The historic swap is corrected as an audited change during the rollout, not edited in place.

10. Lisa Miller and Lisa Miller

Same name and email, different birthdates.

RecordsBorn 1960: CRM 001A000155, registrations R000398 and R000399, chart CH050288. Born 1975: CRM 001A000423. Both CRM accounts carry e-commerce customer 7000300.
Current systemTwo accounts hold the same email and the same e-commerce ID. The order sync takes the first account it finds, so which Lisa receives a purchase depends on record order.
Working prototypeTwo persons. The e-commerce customer matches both on name and email, but joining would put two birthdates in one person, so the cluster rule blocks it and raises a review item at 0.989.
Target designThe reviewer decides which Lisa the customer record belongs to. Until then the two stay apart and no result can be shown to the wrong one.

The company, systems and records in this case study are fictional. The data is treated as protected health information for the design.
