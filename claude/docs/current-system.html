How legacy-sync works today and why it misfiles people

Lumen Health case study. Built from legacy-sync at commit f6bf641 and the eight files in data/.

Every count on this page comes from running the legacy code over the data with claude/analysis/legacy_trace.py, or from the CSV files directly. Each claim says which.

At a glance

Four systems each keep their own copy of the customer. A small Python service, legacy-sync, copies e-commerce orders and portal kit registrations into the CRM. It decides which CRM account an order or a registration belongs to with three lookups: the e-commerce customer ID, the email address, then last name plus zip code.

The last lookup puts purchases on relatives' accounts. Every order then overwrites the account's email. Registration looks people up by email only, so once an email has been overwritten the rightful owner can no longer be found and the CRM rejects them as a duplicate of themselves. Marketing reads its stage from the CRM account, so it misses registrations the CRM rejected or filed elsewhere.

The gift journey

Gifts follow the same path with two people in it. The buyer orders with is_gift set and the kit ships to the recipient. The purchase is filed on the buyer's account, because the sync has no field for a recipient. When the recipient registers, the sync looks them up by the email they typed. 16 of 25 gift registrants used their own email, so the sync finds or creates their account and then moves the purchase across, which fails when that account has an inactive owner. 9 used the buyer's email, so the sync finds the buyer's account, sees a different birthdate and rejects the registration as an identity conflict. In the replay 15 of 25 gift registrations were accepted.

In this data every gift goes to someone with the buyer's surname and zip code. Real gifts will often go to another household, where the surname-and-zip lookup behaves differently again.

What the customer never sees

  • Which CRM account holds their purchase, so a misfiled purchase is invisible until a message or a rejection reveals it.
  • That the CRM rejected their registration while the portal, EHR and lab carried on. In the replay, 24 kits with a final result had no registration in the CRM.
  • That their CRM email flips between family members as each one orders. 19 accounts changed email two or more times.

What the systems resemble

The brief says every system is fictional. The naming still points at familiar products, which helps when reasoning about realistic integration options. Treat this as analogy.

  • The CRM looks like Salesforce. Account IDs start with 001 and opportunity IDs with 006, which are Salesforce's prefixes for those objects. The brief says "person accounts". The code models duplicate rules with block and alert actions, record owners who can be inactive, an integration user and a "Closed Won" stage.
  • E-commerce looks like Shopify. Order names look like #100001, customer IDs are long integers, there is one customer per email and the address columns are named default_address1.

If those analogies hold, the realistic transports are order webhooks or an admin API on the e-commerce side and REST, bulk API or change events on the CRM side. That is a question for the review, with the polling job as the written assumption.

Format quirks that matter for matching

  • Phones come in four formats: 3109602362, 310.960.2362, (310) 960-2362 and +1 310-960-2362. The EHR stores digits only.
  • Emails are already lower case with no plus tags. The variation is dots and wholly different addresses: richardmoore@gmail.com against richard.moore@gmail.com, a work address against a personal one.
  • Nicknames appear in the CRM: Tony for Anthony, Rick for Richard, Bob for Robert, Jess for Jessica, Bill for William, Matt for Matthew, Joe for Joseph, Drew for Andrew, Jen for Jennifer.
  • Relatives share surname and zip: 77 surname and zip pairs cover more than one e-commerce customer, 39 phone numbers are shared by more than one customer and 8 registration emails were used by more than one person.
  • Dates of birth are ISO dates. The CRM uses 1920-01-01 as a placeholder (config.py:15).
  • Booleans are the strings true and false. Amounts are strings with two decimals. Zip codes are five digits. There are seven states.
  • Some columns never vary: channel is always online, stage always Closed Won, created_by always integration_user, status always final. The brief mentions phone sales, but the data has no phone orders.
Three patterns look like artefacts of how the synthetic data was generated. Matching code should not lean on them. Consecutive customer IDs often belong to the same person (15 adjacent pairs). All 30 gift orders ship to someone with the buyer's surname and zip. 129 registrations are timestamped before the kit's delivery.

Workflows

Order sync

Red outlines mark the two steps behind symptoms 1 and 2. Each lookup takes the first account returned when there are several.

  • The docstring at order_sync.py:9-10 says accounts are matched strictly by email. The code below it does something else.
  • The surname comes from the last word of ship_name (order_sync.py:21), so on a gift the lookup uses the recipient's surname.
  • Of the 43 address matches, 14 landed on an account with the buyer's first name and 29 on a different first name. A few of the 29 are nicknames, such as Jessica landing on Jess.
  • In 37 of the 43, the account already carried someone else's e-commerce ID, so the buyer's ID was not stored. That buyer's next order goes through the same guess again.

Kit registration

All three red exits are raised to the caller, so the customer's registration fails. Of the 401 accepted, 391 matched an account by email and 10 created a new one.

Marketing stage and account merge

stage_for_account (marketing/stage.py:6-14) answers for one CRM account: results_ready if any result is attached to it, else registered if any registration is, else bought if it holds an opportunity. The unit is the account. A kit is never looked at on its own.

merge_accounts (consent/merge.py:7-18) deletes the losing account, fills blank fields on the survivor from a fixed list, keeps the loser's email as secondary_email and moves the loser's opportunities across.

The six problems

The six symptoms are mostly one chain. A hotfix in June started guessing accounts by surname and zip. A change in July started overwriting emails. Registration, added between the two, trusts email alone.

One household, five days

This is the legacy code run over real rows from the data, in timestamp order. Elizabeth Hall owns account 001A000417. Thomas Hall shares her surname and zip code 85226.

Orders #100820, #100687 and #100821, registration R000424, result LR000360. In crm_opportunities.csv Thomas's order sits on his own account, which was created three weeks later, so the files show a later state than this replay does.

Purchases show up on a family member's account

Cause
When the e-commerce ID and the email both miss, the order sync takes the first account with the shipment's surname and zip code. It never compares first names.
Where
legacy_sync/sync/order_sync.py:20-24, legacy_sync/crm/client.py:32-34, switched on at legacy_sync/config.py:5. Added by commit 87aff62.
Evidence
Replay: 43 orders matched this way, 29 onto an account with a different first name. CRM file: 23 opportunities sit on an account whose first name differs from the buyer's. About 13 of those are a relative and 9 are a nickname of the same person. Order #100021 from Mary Rodriguez is on Kevin Rodriguez's account while #100050 from Kevin is on Mary's.

Email addresses in the CRM change unexpectedly

Cause
After every order, matched or not, the sync writes the order's email and phone over the account's.
Where
legacy_sync/sync/order_sync.py:42, legacy_sync/crm/client.py:48-55. Added by commit 11cc1ab.
Evidence
Replay: 77 orders changed an email, 43 of them after an address match. 19 accounts changed two or more times, flipping between two people's addresses as each one orders. CRM file: 15 accounts hold an email that differs from their linked e-commerce customer's. In the file those 15 are all the same person under a second address.

Kit registrations rejected as a possible duplicate

Cause
Registration looks for the account by email only. When that misses, it tries to create an account and the CRM's duplicate rule blocks any create whose first name, last name and zip match an existing account. The existing account is usually the registrant's own.
Where
legacy_sync/registration/register.py:28 and :36-43, legacy_sync/crm/client.py:38-40, legacy_sync/crm/duplicate_rules.py:4-11, legacy_sync/config.py:9-12. Added by commit f4adca0.
Evidence
Replay: 20 blocked. In 16 the blocking account used to hold the registrant's email until an order overwrote it. In the other 4 the registrant used an email the CRM never had. A further 11 are rejected as identity conflicts: the email belongs to an account with the same surname and a different birthdate. 6 of those accounts got that email from an overwrite.

Gift registrations fail and support cannot move the purchase

Cause
A gift purchase is filed on the buyer's account and moved to the recipient only when the recipient registers. The move is refused when the recipient's account belongs to an inactive owner. The exception fails the whole registration. Support hits the same check. The CRM has a gift_recipient_account_id column that nothing uses.
Where
legacy_sync/registration/register.py:45-46, legacy_sync/crm/client.py:57-61.
Evidence
Replay: 10 of 25 gift registrations are rejected: 1 on an inactive owner, 4 by the duplicate rule and 5 by identity conflict, against 22 of 408 for other registrations. 54 of 689 accounts have an inactive owner, so the single failure understates the exposure. R000459: Joseph Harris registers a gift from Lisa Harris, his account is owned by inactive user U007 and the purchase stays on Lisa's account.

"Please register your kit" sent to customers who have results

Cause
The stage is worked out per CRM account. The purchase can sit on one account while the registration and result sit on another, or the CRM can have rejected the registration while the portal, EHR and lab carried on. Only gift purchases are ever moved to the registrant.
Where
legacy_sync/marketing/stage.py:6-14, legacy_sync/registration/register.py:45.
Evidence
Replay: of 406 kits with a final result, the purchasing account reads results_ready for 346, bought for 59 and registered for 1. Of the 60 wrong ones, 36 have the registration on another account, 15 were blocked by the duplicate rule and 9 by identity conflict. Nothing in the repo calls the stage helper, so I attached each registration and result to the account that registration resolved to. That wiring is my assumption.

Text opt-outs lost when duplicate accounts are merged

Cause
The merge copies a fixed list of fields from the losing account. sms_opt_out and do_not_call are not on the list, so the survivor's values stand and the loser's opt-out is deleted with it.
Where
legacy_sync/consent/merge.py:3-4 and :9-12. Added by commit 444e526.
Evidence
Read from the code. The replay never merges. CRM file: 58 emails are held by more than one account and 3 of those pairs disagree on both consent flags. Accounts 001A000126 and 001A000560 are both Matthew Anderson, one opted out and one not. Merging into the second would opt him back in.

Other findings

These are not among the six symptoms. They matter for the fix or for the live code review.

  • The address fallback cannot be switched off by itself. With MATCH_FALLBACK_ADDRESS off, 16 orders raise DuplicateDetected inside sync_order, which replay.py:30 does not catch. 11 kit registrations then have no opportunity to attach to. The fallback is hiding the duplicate rule from the order path. My guess is that this was the "June backlog" the hotfix names.
  • A test protects the bug. tests/test_order_sync.py:24-28 asserts that Ana's order lands on Carlos's account and calls it reusing the household account. Fixing symptom 1 means changing that test.
  • Registrations can be accepted onto the wrong person silently. When the account's birthdate is blank or the placeholder, _same_person compares surnames only (register.py:17-18). Accounts created by the order sync always have a blank birthdate (order_sync.py:34). In the replay 11 registrations were accepted onto an account with a different first name. 7 of them passed on surname alone, such as R000465 for Nina Martinez accepted onto Mary Martinez's account.
  • The first match wins everywhere: order_sync.py:14, :18, :24 and register.py:30. In the replay 39 registrations matched more than one account by email and 10 address matches had more than one candidate.
  • Nothing is atomic or idempotent. Registration can create an account (register.py:36) and then fail on the move (register.py:46) with no rollback. Running an order twice creates two opportunities.
  • Lookups ignore phone, although store.py:18-20 can normalise it. Email normalising is trim and lower case, so richardmoore@ and richard.moore@ are different people to the sync.
  • The unfinished matcher should not be adopted as it stands. Two missing phones or two missing emails count as a match (matcher_v1.py:17-19). Without an email match the best possible score is 0.70 against a threshold of 0.75. A father and son with the same name who share an email and a phone score 0.80 and match.
  • The replay is a reconstruction. It seeds from accounts created before 2026-06-15 and ends with 629 accounts where the CRM file has 689. Of 726 opportunities that can be compared, 704 land on the same account. 85 opportunities in the file sit on an account created after the opportunity, so the files are an end state that includes changes we cannot see.
  • There is no logging, no audit trail and no access control anywhere in the service. Exception messages carry account and registration IDs but no names.

Where the health information is

The brief says to treat the data as protected health information.

  • ehr_patients.csv and lab_results.csv are clinical records. The results file has a status but no values.
  • portal_registrations.csv ties a name, birthdate, email and phone to a specific test kit.
  • Orders and CRM opportunities name the product, so "Genetic Wellness Kit" next to a person is health information once it is linked to an identity.
  • A marketing stage of results_ready tells whoever receives it that the person took a test.
  • Today the CRM is where all of this meets, with sales reps as record owners.

Constraints the current design creates

  • Registration depends on the CRM being reachable and willing. The brief's fixed rule is that registration must never wait on identity, which is the opposite of register.py:23-24.
  • The CRM is being replaced. Identity that lives in CRM account IDs and CRM duplicate rules will not survive that.
  • The CRM's own rules reach into the customer journey: a duplicate rule set to block and an ownership rule can each stop a kit registration.
  • A purchase has one account slot. Buyer and recipient cannot both be recorded, so gifts need a move that can fail.
  • Consent is two flags on an account, with no channel address, timestamp or source.
