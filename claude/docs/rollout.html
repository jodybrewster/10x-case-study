90-day rollout

90-day rollout: from the current sync to one customer identity

Lumen Health case study. How the identity design in the target architecture is introduced while orders and registrations keep flowing and the CRM is replaced, written against the findings in how legacy-sync works today.

The fixed constraint is that a customer's kit registration is never blocked waiting on identity. The first two weeks make that true without any new service. Everything after that adds identity behind flags, one consumer at a time.

Summary

The rollout has four phases over 90 days. Phase 0 (days 1 to 14) stops the damage in legacy-sync and the registration path without introducing anything new. Phase 1 (days 15 to 45) runs the identity service in shadow mode and proves its matching on a hand-labelled set. Phase 2 (days 46 to 75) switches consumers to the identity result one at a time, each behind a flag. Phase 3 (days 76 to 90) keys the replacement CRM on person_key and retires legacy-sync.

A kit registration is never blocked waiting on identity or on the CRM. That holds from the first week and stays true in every later phase.

Two milestones can look alike and are different. The week 1 to 2 change is an immediate registration-path decoupling: the portal stops depending on the CRM, so a CRM failure can no longer reject a customer. It ships in Phase 0 and needs no identity service. The later milestone, days 46 to 75, is consuming identity results: the CRM and the portal start receiving and storing person_key from the service. The first removes a dependency. The second adds a consumer. The first never waits for the second.

The repository supplied with the case is a local CSV replay with an in-memory CRM stand-in. Every code reference below points into that replay. How production reaches these systems is not known, so each step that touches a production system carries an assumption in the dependencies section.

What never changes during rollout

  • Orders keep flowing through the existing sync until the consumer that replaces it has matched its decisions for a full cycle. No phase turns the order feed off.
  • A kit registration is accepted the moment the customer submits it. After Phase 0 no CRM outcome and no identity outcome can reject it.
  • EHR chart creation stays as it is, from name and birthdate. The EHR gains an identifier in Phase 2 and no new workflow.
  • The lab integration and the result-by-barcode flow are untouched.
  • Nothing clinical crosses to the commercial side. Systems outside the health-data boundary receive the key, contact details, consent and a kit stage only.
  • Nothing is deleted to create identity. Every merge is an audited event that can be undone.
  • The files in data/ and the supplied legacy-sync history are evidence and are not edited. Every fix below is a change to make in the live service.

Order of work inside the phase

Change (c) and change (f) ship first, by day 7, because they protect customers directly and depend on nothing else. Changes (d) and (e) follow with no customer-facing effect. Changes (a) and (b) ship last, by day 14, with (a) as one release so the duplicate rule and the fallback never differ. A replay of the supplied data with the changes applied is the acceptance test: the 16 uncaught duplicate errors must be zero.

Reliability contract for the registration change

The adversarial review asked for an explicit contract behind "registration never waits". The assumption here is that the portal has its own durable store. If it does not, the first task of Phase 0 is to add one.

  • Acceptance boundary: the registration is accepted when the portal has written it to its own store. The customer sees success at that moment.
  • The portal writes a work item in the same transaction as the registration. The registration ID is the idempotency key, so a double submit or a retry creates one record and one CRM action.
  • A worker calls the CRM after acceptance. A rejected call is retried with growing delays. After a fixed number of attempts the item moves to a dead-letter list that support works by hand.
  • Replay: any item or range of items can be re-run by registration ID. A repeated run is safe.
  • If the work queue is unavailable the registration is still stored. A nightly sweep finds stored registrations with no CRM action and creates the missing items.

Phase 1: identity service in shadow mode, days 15 to 45

The service reads events and makes decisions. It writes nothing to production systems. Its output is compared with legacy-sync every day, so the first time anyone relies on it is after its errors have been counted.

ItemDetail
GoalShow with measured numbers that the matcher is safe to trust. Have a staffed review queue ready before any production system consumes a result.
Changes shippedEvent ingest for orders, registrations and results (feed or webhook, whichever production offers). The matcher and the identity store described in the target architecture. The review queue and its screen, with reason codes and no names in the list view. The append-only event log. A daily comparison report of identity decisions against legacy-sync placement. A hand-labelled validation set.
Who is affectedSupport, who staff the review queue. Engineering, who run the comparison. Clinical operations, who are told how chart-pair review items will reach them. Customers and every production system are unaffected. The service has read access to events only.
Entry criteriaG1 passed. A named owner and at least two trained reviewers for the queue. The privacy officer has approved the store, its access scopes and its logging as part of the health-data boundary.
Exit criteriaGate G3 on day 45. The validation set holds at least 600 labelled automatic links, drawn as a simple random sample of every record pair at or above the threshold and labelled independently by two reviewers, with disagreements settled by a third. Separate random samples of the review queue and of pairs just under the threshold are labelled as well and are reported separately. Auto-link precision and false-match rate are reported with a 95 percent interval. With no false match in 600 labelled automatic links the upper bound of the Wilson interval is about 0.6 percent, which is why 600 is the floor. Every rule family without enough labelled pairs is set to review and does not auto-link. The queue has run for at least 14 days with its oldest open item under 2 working days. The daily comparison has an explained cause for every difference.
RollbackStop the ingest and switch the service off. Nothing in production depends on it, so there is nothing to undo. The store is kept for analysis.
RisksThe labelled set is not representative, so precision is overstated. Reviewers are not available and the queue backs up. Event feeds arrive late or incomplete in production, which skews the comparison. Review screens expose more health information than the role needs.

Rules that hold until the numbers exist

  • Until auto-link precision and the false-match rate have been measured on the validation set, the uncertain rule families go to review and do not auto-link. These are the ones the adversarial review named: plus-tag removal in email, last-ten-digit phone comparison across country codes and the birthdate transposition exception.
  • The 0.90 and 0.60 thresholds in the target architecture are starting values. G3 is the first moment a threshold is trusted. It is trusted only for the rule families that passed.
  • The kit barcode links are deterministic and need no threshold. Every other family waits for its measurement, including exact email with a matching birthdate.

Daily comparison

Each day the report lists orders where the identity service would have placed the opportunity on a different person than legacy-sync did, registrations the service would resolve differently and the review items opened. Differences are sorted into three causes: the service is right, legacy-sync is right or both are wrong. Only the second and third cause lead to a change in the matcher.

Phase 2: consume identity results, days 46 to 75

This is the later milestone that the summary separates from the Phase 0 decoupling. Consumers start reading the identity result. Each cutover sits behind its own flag, runs in parallel with the old behaviour first and has a written rollback.

ItemDetail
GoalMove the decision about who a person is out of the CRM and into the identity service, one consumer at a time, starting with the most visible failure.
Who is affectedMarketing, whose sends change from per-account stage to per-kit and per-person stage with consent per channel. CRM administrators and reps, who see person_key on accounts and a pending-review flag. Portal and EHR teams, who add a field. Support, who run historic clean-up through the review screen. Customers see fewer wrong messages and nothing else.
Entry criteriaG3 passed. A flag exists for each cutover. Change (d) from Phase 0 has run in production. The vendor terms for the marketing platform are settled (see dependencies).
Exit criteriaGate G7 on day 75. All three consumers are on the flag set to identity. The CRM no longer decides identity: its duplicate rule only alerts and its account is a view of a person. Sends to kits with a registration or a result are zero. Every account linked above threshold carries a person_key and the rest are listed in the queue. Every historic merge in the audit log has an actor, a reason and a working undo.
RollbackPer flag, in under one working day: the consumer returns to its previous input. Marketing returns to the suppression rule from Phase 0. The CRM ignores person_key and runs on the Phase 0 sync. The portal and EHR keep the stored key, which is harmless. A merge is undone from the audit log.
RisksA wrong auto-link now reaches a customer. A marketing vendor that is not covered by the right agreement receives a stage word. Custom fields on the production CRM or the EHR turn out not to be possible. Reviewers cannot keep up with clean-up volume next to day-to-day items.

Historic clean-up

Clean-up starts on day 60 in batches, after change (d) has shipped and the CRM carries the key. It never edits an account in place.

  • Purchases filed on a relative's account are moved by an audited identity merge or a role correction. A relative is split out as a separate person with a household relationship. The replay shows 43 orders matched by surname and zip, 29 of them onto an account with a different first name.
  • Duplicate accounts are merged through the service, with the strictest consent value kept for each channel address.
  • Each batch is sampled before it is applied. A batch is applied only if the sample shows no false merge. Every merge carries an actor, a reason and an undo.
  • Accounts held by inactive owners are handled in the same flow. Identity links and role changes never check ownership.

Phase 3: CRM replacement and retirement, days 76 to 90

The replacement CRM starts keyed on person_key, so it does not inherit the account-as-person model. The current CRM and the new one run as consumers of the same identity events.

ItemDetail
GoalCut over to the replacement CRM without recreating the identity problems. Retire legacy-sync once the identity consumer has matched it for a full cycle.
Changes shippedThe replacement CRM keyed on person_key from its first record. Dual-run: both CRMs consume the same events and a daily diff compares them. A migration mapping from old account_id to person_key, built from the identity store and kept after cutover. legacy-sync retired. Flags and the comparison job removed after the retirement.
Who is affectedReps and support, who work in the new CRM. Marketing and the portal, which read the key and do not change. The CRM vendor or implementation team, who must accept the key as a required field. Customers are unaffected.
Entry criteriaG7 passed on day 75. The replacement CRM has a staging instance that accepts a person key field and event-driven writes. The mapping has been run on a copy and every account either maps to a key or sits in the review queue.
Exit criteriaGate G8 on day 90. Dual-run has produced 14 consecutive days with zero unexplained differences between the two CRMs for opportunity placement and consent. The identity consumer has matched legacy-sync placement for one full cycle, which is assumed to mean 14 consecutive days after the CRM cutover on day 61 with every difference explained. The mapping covers every active account. Nothing reads from legacy-sync.
RollbackBefore retirement, legacy-sync can be switched back on as the order feed within one working day, because it has run in shadow and kept its state. Before the final CRM cutover, users return to the old CRM, which is still receiving events. After retirement the rollback is a restore of the last legacy-sync release from version control, accepted as slower.
RisksThe replacement schedule does not match these days. The new CRM cannot hold an external key. A diff between the two CRMs is dominated by field naming and hides real differences. Users switch late and keep working in the old CRM after cutover, so their edits go to a system that is no longer fed.

If the replacement CRM date is different

The schedule is unknown, so the plan bends in two directions. If the replacement arrives later than day 90, Phase 3 reduces to retiring legacy-sync and the current CRM keeps its key. If it arrives earlier, Phase 2 step 2 targets the new CRM directly and the old CRM is fed only until its own retirement. Either way nothing in Phase 0 or Phase 1 changes.

What remains after day 90

  • Decommissioning the old CRM after its dual-run window, on the replacement schedule.
  • The review queue as steady-state work with an owner, a daily age target and a monthly recalibration from the decisions.
  • Review of suspected duplicate EHR charts by clinical operations. That never reaches code.
  • Rule families still in review because their labelled set was small. Each one is promoted to auto-link only by a new measurement and a gate decision.
  • Retention, audit access reviews and the vendor agreement renewal cycle.
  • Phone sales and other entry points outside the extract, which raise the same event an order does.

Dependencies and open questions

The supplied repository is a local CSV replay with an in-memory CRM stand-in. How orders, registrations and results reach each system in production is not established by it. The assumptions below are written down so they can be corrected early.

QuestionAssumed here
How do orders reach legacy-sync in production and how late?A polling job, up to 30 minutes behind. The identity service reads the same feed or a webhook with the same event shape. Phase 1 needs one of the two
Does the portal call registration synchronously and does it have its own store?Yes to the call, as the code reads. The portal has a durable store. If not, adding one is the first task of Phase 0
Where does the production CRM keep additional contacts and can it hold a custom key field?It can hold a custom field for person_key and an additional email. The replay uses secondary_email
What can the marketing platform do?It can suppress by a rule on a kit barcode attribute and take a daily feed of key, stage and consent. Vendor name and capabilities are inferred only
Is the marketing platform covered by the right agreement?Not known. If it is not, it receives the registered stage only and never a results-ready stage
What replaces the CRM and when?Phased, with the new CRM ready for dual-run from about day 76. A different date is handled as described in Phase 3
Can the EHR and the portal store an external person key?Yes, as an extra field with no change to chart creation. If not, the mapping lives in the identity store
Who staffs the review queue and with how many people?Support, at least two trained reviewers, with a 2 working day age target
What counts as a full cycle for legacy-sync retirement?14 consecutive days with every daily difference explained. Production volume may justify a longer cycle, such as one month end
How large is the real population?Unknown. The sample holds about 2,000 customer records. The 600 labelled automatic links are a floor for the false-match bound and are revisited against real volumes
Are phone sales in scope?They exist outside the extract. Rep-created accounts raise the same event an order does, from the day the CRM consumes identity events
